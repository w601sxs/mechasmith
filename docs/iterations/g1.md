# Unitree G1: implement → critique → improve → reimplement

Reference: MuJoCo Menagerie `unitree_g1` (29 DOF, 33.3 kg): tilted hip roll, hip yaw and
shoulder pitch axes; a 3-joint waist whose roll and pitch axes cross; five motor classes
(139, 88, 50, 25, 5 N·m), position servos with kp 500 and dampratio 1; two IMUs with noise and
cutoffs; a `stand` keyframe.

## Round 1: the library as it was after the Go2 ([g1_round1.py](g1_round1.py))

| problem | what happened |
|---|---|
| mirroring | the right hip roll and shoulder roll ranges were not mirrored, and the right shoulder pitch axis came out 32° off: Unitree's convention is per joint, and `same` kept both axis and range |
| structure | every link drawn by hand as a motor hub plus a strut: 32 overlaps at home, worst where axes cross (waist, ankles) |
| mass | exact only because every link's mass was copied onto a hand-drawn part; no motors, no materials |
| actuators | kv set by hand (Menagerie uses dampratio=1); no field for sensor noise or cutoff |

## Library changes

- `asm.chain(parent, [ChainJoint(...), ...])`: limbs from a joint table, the way a URDF
  describes them. Each joint's motor goes on the parent side and its output flange on the child
  side; struts leave the flange along the axis, go out past the motor's radius, then run to the
  next joint, so nothing overlaps by construction. `hub=None` makes a cross piece (crossing axes).
- Mass budgets: `Component.mass_budget` / `ChainJoint(mass=)` says what a whole link weighs; its
  structure takes whatever the motors and other parts leave. Resolved at export, after mirroring,
  with a note when the other parts already exceed the budget (it caught a wrist link that would
  have held a heavier motor than the whole link weighs).
- `Robot.mirror(axes="closest")` (`same` is now an alias): each joint is reflected as a direction or
  as a rotation axis, whichever keeps its axis closest to the original; ranges follow. Gives
  Unitree's convention for both the Go2 and the G1.
- `ActuatorModel(dampratio=)` for position servos; `asm.imu(..., noise=, cutoff=)`.
- Pin rule in the interference check: a hinge's pin may overlap the two parts it joins (and, through
  a cross piece, the part beyond) within its radius; anywhere else they must not.
- MJCF excludes contact between the bodies on either side of a cross piece.
- Fixes: placing a robot on the floor used the bounding-sphere radius of primitive collision shapes
  (the G1 started 84 mm in the air, the Go2 100 mm); now exact per shape. The CLI's stdout carries
  only the JSON report (OpenCascade warnings printed from C++ used to corrupt it).

## Round 2 ([designs/g1.py](../../designs/g1.py))

- All 29 joint axes on the reference's, every sign and range equal (left and right).
- Mass per link equal to the reference's (the measured masses are the budgets, so this is by
  construction; the independent check is inertia: pelvis +9 to +57%, torso +16 to +50%, the
  motors sit further out than the real, more compact joints).
- No collisions at home. Joint ranges are limited only by body contacts in the stand pose (hands
  on the hips, thighs on the torso at the extremes).
- Stand test on the position servos: 0.0° pitch, 0.7 mm settle, stiffness ratio 1.46. The
  reference in the same test: −0.1°, 0.7 mm, 1.47.
- Two IMUs with Menagerie's noise; `home` keyframe; URDF matches the MJCF.
